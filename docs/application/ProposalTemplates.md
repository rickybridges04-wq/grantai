# ProposalTemplates

**Status:** Draft — founder review required
**Phase:** 5 — Application System

## Purpose

Holds the structural templates `GrantWriter-Agent` selects from when
drafting a narrative, and `DocumentGeneration.md` assembles into final
output. Templates are structure, not content — the actual language comes
from Writer pulling business-specific facts from `BusinessProfiles.md` and
`CommercializationPlans.md`.

## Standard Template Sections

Most grant narratives share a common backbone, even when funders use
different section names for it:

1. **Executive summary** — the ask, in brief
2. **Organization background** — who the business is, relevant history
3. **Need/problem statement** — why this funding matters now
4. **Project description** — what will actually be done with the funds
5. **Goals and outcomes** — how success is measured
6. **Budget narrative** — ties to `BudgetTemplates.md`, explains the numbers
7. **Timeline**
8. **Capacity/qualifications** — why this business can execute
9. **Sustainability** — what happens after the grant period ends (common
   requirement, easy to forget)

## Template Variants

- **Federal** — typically the most rigid section structure and page limits;
  template should track the exact section order/naming a given federal
  program requires
- **State** — usually similar backbone, often more flexible on order
- **Corporate/Foundation** — frequently shorter-form; some are letter-style
  rather than full-proposal — needs a distinct condensed template rather
  than a trimmed-down federal one
